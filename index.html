An idea from the 432Hz trend – what if you could retune your computer’s entire audio output to your liking? YBK AUDIO’s light app 432Hz does that in a very simple and easy way.

Open the spectrum graphs to see the frequency change as the numbers go down and up. This app is great both for visual analysis, teaching and everyday listening.

Who knows, maybe hearing everything in 432Hz really is good for you.

Explore each feature

Whole PC

Every app, at once

Whatever your computer plays to your speakers or headphones is retuned, from the browser and music player to games, video calls and your DAW. It works inside the system’s own audio, so there’s nothing to set up in each app.

Compare

Before and after, one click

The big button switches between your new pitch and the original while the music keeps playing, with no click or gap. Left-click the gold ring by the clock to do the same from the tray.

Any pitch

Any pitch you want

Set A4 anywhere from 1 Hz to 20,000 Hz: step with − and + (hold to repeat), or double-click the number and type it. Prefer to think in C? Tune by C4 instead.

Spectrum

See where your tuning sits

Open the spectrum and every A (or C) of your tuning is marked, from the lowest octave to the highest. Maximise the window and it fills the screen.

In real time

Right on pitch

Every speaker’s clock runs a little fast or slow. 432hz measures yours and corrects for it, so the pitch comes out exactly where you set it. At A4 = 432, a 440 Hz tone really becomes 432 Hz.

Out of the way

Out of sight

Close the window and 432hz keeps going from the tray (the menu bar on a Mac). Turn on Start with Windows (Open at login on a Mac) and every session starts at your pitch. Uninstall it and your sound is back exactly as it was.

The idea came from a mixing course – what if we could Visualize the locations of each sound in a mix? The pad at the back, the bass and kicks at the bottom, mono and stereo instruments kicking in right and left, the vocals. We were working on that idea when we came up with an idea to extend a stereometer by frequency.

You can find stereo imaging anywhere. But not in 3D.

Visualize proposes a new way of stereo image analysis. From a cylinder-shaped Vectorscope stacked up every frequency, to a monitoring environment that measures Mono loss, we provide groundbreaking 3D graphs you can use to analyze a song’s phasing.

Explore each feature

3D · Vector

A vectorscope for every frequency

One vectorscope per frequency, stacked up into a cylinder from bass to highs. The front is mono, the sides are left and right only, the back is sound that cancels in mono. Drag to turn it.

3D · Room

Your two speakers in a room

See where each sound appears between your speakers, and what the speakers fill the room with.

3D · Image

Every sound where it sits in the mix

Each sound is a cloud at its place in the mix: left and right is its pan, height is its pitch, the front is loud, and a wide cloud means its left and right are unalike.

3D · Image + Stems

See the instruments apart

Switch the colours to STEMS and an AI colours every graph by instrument: vocals yellow, drums blue, bass violet, everything else green. It runs about a second behind the music.

3D · Monitor

Check what mono will lose

Every frequency, measured: left and right is its level balance, height is its pitch, the back is what gets lost when the mix is summed to mono. Inside the faint target shape is mono-safe; dots outside it stand out.

Notes, key, chords

Know what’s playing

The loudest peaks are labelled with their note, like “F1 · 44 Hz”. One click listens for about 8 seconds and estimates the song’s key; a chord box then shows the chord playing now.

FAQs

Installing, your license code, and how to reach us.

Seeing “Windows protected your PC”?

When you open the Instrument Player installer, Windows may show a blue box that says “Windows protected your PC” and “Unknown publisher”. That is Windows SmartScreen. It appears for new apps that aren’t signed with a code-signing certificate yet and haven’t built up a history with Microsoft. Instrument Player is new, and our certificate is still being issued, so for now Windows can’t tell who made it.

To install it anyway, click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Only do this with the installer you downloaded from ybkaudio.com or from the link in our email.

This has nothing to do with your license. Your code only counts when you type it into the app. Seeing this warning, closing it, or downloading the installer again never uses up a license, so there is no need to ask for a new code.

Lost your license code?

Search your inbox for an email from license@ybkaudio.com. It has your code and the download link.

Got it free during the launch month? Enter the same email on the Instrument Player page again and we’ll send the same code again.

Bought it? Email us from the address you paid with and include the order number from your Paddle receipt.

Windows says “This app can’t run on your PC”? That almost always means the download was cut short. Compare the file with the size shown under the Download button; if it is smaller, download it again. Antivirus software and unstable Wi-Fi are the usual causes.

How licenses work

  • One-time purchase. You pay once. There is no subscription.
  • One computer at a time. A code runs on one computer at a time. It can go onto another computer once it has been released from the first.
  • Moving to a new computer. On the old computer, open Settings in the app and press Release license. Then enter your code on the new computer. If the old computer has broken down or is gone, write to us and we’ll release the license for you. A license can be moved once every 30 days.
  • What doesn’t need a move. Reinstalling Windows on the same computer, app updates, and swapping the drive, memory or graphics card. A new motherboard or CPU counts as a new computer: move the license to it as above.
  • Your projects and settings come too. Instrument Player keeps everything you make and set up in one folder: Documents\YBK AUDIO\Instrument Player. Copy that folder to the new computer before you start the app there, and everything will be where you left it.
  • Free launch licenses. They move like any other license, and on any computer they run the versions released during the free month, up to 19 October 2026. Download the last of them →
