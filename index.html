No one said idea generation had to start in a heavy DAW. Instrument Player helps you get closer to your instruments without having to enter work mode all the time.

It remembers what you play. The app provides MIDI replay, which brings back the last 5 minutes of your playing. Load up your sudden ideas and make small edits via the MIDI editor, and export the .mid file for later use.

Why get it

  • Any VST3 instrument, on its own — no DAW, no project, no track to set up.
  • Nothing you play is lost — MIDI Replay keeps the last 5 minutes, without pressing Record.
  • From idea to .mid — tidy it in the piano roll and export it for your DAW.
  • No controller needed — press M and your computer keyboard plays like a piano.
  • Light and quick — low CPU, and roughly 5–10 ms in WASAPI exclusive mode.
Find

Find and play your VST instrument

Every VST3 instrument on your PC in one searchable list, effects filtered out and favourites pinned to the top. Pick one and it opens at its own size, ready to play.

Effects

Add any VST audio effect

Chain your own VST3 effects after the instrument, in the order the sound runs through them. The list tells you what kind each effect is.

MIDI Replay

All MIDI input replayable

The last 5 minutes of your playing are always in memory — nothing to arm, nothing to press. What you played while warming up is still there, exactly as you played it.

Piano roll

Edit and export MIDI

Quantise, copy, paste, duplicate, box select and zoom on a tempo grid, then export a standard .mid file for your DAW. MIDI — the notes you played — not audio.

No controller?

Use your computer keyboard as a MIDI controller

Press M and your keyboard becomes a piano: one row, or two rows for over two octaves, with Z and X or [ and ] to change octave, or keys of your own. Every real MIDI input still works at the same time, including ones plugged in while the app is running.

Performance

Low CPU and latency

CPU and latency sit in the toolbar while you play. WASAPI exclusive mode runs at roughly 5–10 ms, set under the gear icon.

Whole PC

Every app, at once

Your browser, music player, games, video calls, your DAW — whatever your computer plays to your speakers or headphones is retuned. It works inside the system’s own audio, so there’s nothing to set up in each app.

Compare

Before and after, one click

The big button switches between your new pitch and the original while the music keeps playing — no click, no gap. Left-click the gold ring by the clock to do the same from the tray.

Any pitch

Any pitch you want

Set A4 anywhere from 1 Hz to 20,000 Hz: step with − and + (hold to repeat), or double-click the number and type it. Prefer to think in C? Tune by C4 instead.

Spectrum

See where your tuning sits

Open the spectrum and every A (or C) of your tuning is marked, from the lowest octave to the highest. Maximise the window and it fills the screen.

In real time

Right on pitch

Every speaker’s clock runs a little fast or slow. 432hz measures yours and corrects for it, so the pitch comes out exactly where you set it — a 440 Hz tone really becomes 432 Hz at A4 = 432.

Out of the way

Out of sight

Close the window and 432hz keeps going from the tray (the menu bar on a Mac). Turn on Start with Windows (Open at login on a Mac) and every session starts at your pitch. Uninstall it and your sound is back exactly as it was.

432hz — specification
TypeDesktop app with a tray / menu-bar icon · changes the pitch of all system audio
PitchAny A4 from 1 to 20,000 Hz, or set by C4 · tempo unchanged · starts at A4 = 432 Hz (−31.77 cents)
AccuracyWithin two semitones of 440 (432 included): within 0.2 cents from 41 Hz to 4 kHz · bigger shifts: within about 4 cents, though far downward shifts can leave the lowest notes up to 35 cents off · speaker clock drift corrected
DelayOn Windows: about 10–20 ms within two semitones of 440, about 120 ms for bigger shifts · CPU about 1.5–2%
Works withWindows: apps that play through Windows’ normal (shared) audio — ASIO and exclusive-mode audio bypass Windows’ audio engine and are not tuned. Mac: what macOS plays, through its system-audio tap.
InstallWindows: one administrator prompt for the Windows audio step · the sound drops for a few seconds while it installs · speakers added later (new USB or Bluetooth) are retuned after running the installer again. Mac: drag 432hz into Applications · allow System Audio Recording when asked
UninstallWindows: Settings > Apps — your speakers go back to how they were. Mac: quit it from the menu bar and drag it to the Trash; it changes nothing in the system

CompressorOuter ring sets threshold (0 to −60 dB); inner ring raises ratio (1:1 to 10:1) and make-up gain together. Over-threshold energy glows red on a live spectrum.

ReverbOne knob grows the room size and the reverb level together.

3D · Vector

A vectorscope for every frequency

One vectorscope per frequency, stacked up into a cylinder from bass to highs. The front is mono, the sides are left and right only, the back is sound that cancels in mono. Drag to turn it.

3D · Room

Your two speakers in a room

See where each sound appears between your speakers, and what the speakers fill the room with.

3D · Image

Every sound where it sits in the mix

Each sound is a cloud at its place in the mix: left and right is its pan, height is its pitch, the front is loud, and a wide cloud means its left and right are unalike.

3D · Image + Stems

See the instruments apart

Switch the colours to STEMS and an AI colours every graph by instrument: vocals yellow, drums blue, bass violet, everything else green. It runs about a second behind the music.

3D · Monitor

Check what mono will lose

Every frequency, measured: left and right is its level balance, height is its pitch, the back is what gets lost when the mix is summed to mono. Inside the faint target shape is mono-safe; dots outside it stand out.

Notes, key, chords

Know what’s playing

The loudest peaks are labelled with their note, like “F1 · 44 Hz”. One click listens for about 8 seconds and estimates the song’s key; a chord box then shows the chord playing now and its place in the key. Loudness (LUFS, true peak) and the spectrum are always on screen.

Help

Installing, your license code, and how to reach us.

Seeing “Windows protected your PC”?

When you open the Instrument Player installer, Windows may show a blue box that says “Windows protected your PC” and “Unknown publisher”. That is Windows SmartScreen. It appears for new apps that aren’t signed with a code-signing certificate yet and haven’t built up a history with Microsoft. Instrument Player is new, and our certificate is still being issued, so for now Windows can’t tell who made it.

To install it anyway, click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Only do this with the installer you downloaded from ybkaudio.com or from the link in our email.

This has nothing to do with your license. Your code only counts when you type it into the app. Seeing this warning, closing it, or downloading the installer again never uses up a license, so there is no need to ask for a new code.

Lost your license code?

Search your inbox for an email from license@ybkaudio.com. It has your code and the download link.

Got it free during the launch month? Enter the same email on the Instrument Player page again and we’ll send the same code again.

Bought it? Email us from the address you paid with and include the order number from your Paddle receipt.

Windows says “This app can’t run on your PC”? That almost always means the download was cut short. Compare the file with the size shown under the Download button; if it is smaller, download it again — antivirus software and unstable Wi-Fi are the usual causes.
