Instrument Player runs the virtual instruments you already own as their own app. A lot of great synths only ship as plugins — so trying a sound or playing for five minutes means booting a DAW and making a track. Instrument Player removes the timeline, the tracks and the mixer, and keeps only the part you wanted: one instrument, ready to play.

It finds the VST3 instruments on your PC, shows the plugin’s own interface at exactly its size, and takes input from every MIDI controller you plug in — or from your computer keyboard.

And it never misses the take. MIDI Replay keeps the last 5 minutes of your playing in memory without you pressing Record, so the phrase you stumbled into can be pulled back, tidied up in the piano roll and exported as a .mid file for your DAW. What leaves the app is MIDI — the notes you played — not audio.

Find

Find and play your VST instrument

Every VST3 instrument on your PC in one searchable list, effects filtered out and favourites pinned to the top. Pick one and it opens at its own size, ready to play.

Effects

Add any VST audio effect

Chain your own VST3 effects after the instrument, in the order the sound runs through them. The list tells you what kind each effect is.

MIDI Replay

All MIDI input replayable

The last 5 minutes of your playing are always in memory — nothing to arm, nothing to press. What you played while warming up is still there, exactly as you played it.

Piano roll

Edit and export MIDI

Quantise, copy, paste, duplicate, box select and zoom on a tempo grid, then export a standard .mid file for your DAW. MIDI — the notes you played — not audio.

No controller?

Use your computer keyboard as a MIDI controller

Press M and your keyboard becomes a piano: one row, or two rows for over two octaves, with Z and X or [ and ] to change octave, or keys of your own. Every real MIDI input still works at the same time, including ones plugged in while the app is running.

Performance

Low CPU and latency

CPU and latency sit in the toolbar while you play. WASAPI exclusive mode runs at roughly 5–10 ms, set under the gear icon.

432hz changes the pitch of everything your PC plays, live, to whatever you choose — without changing the tempo. Set A4 to any frequency and every app follows: your browser, music player, games, video calls, your DAW.

The most common reason to do this is A4 = 432 Hz, and that’s where the app gets its name. Almost all music is tuned to A4 = 440 Hz; some musicians and listeners prefer 432 Hz, a little lower. But 432 is just one setting: tune up or down, match an instrument tuned to a different pitch, or try any tuning you’re curious about.

On Windows it runs inside Windows’ own audio engine as an audio effect; on a Mac it uses macOS’s own system-audio tap. Either way there’s no driver or virtual cable to set up. Changes within two semitones of 440 — 432 included — use an exact engine: every note from 41 Hz to 4 kHz lands within 0.2 cents, with about 10–20 ms of delay on Windows. Bigger changes switch to a second engine on their own, which adds delay (about 120 ms on Windows).

Whole PC

Every app, at once

Your browser, music player, games, video calls, your DAW — whatever your computer plays to your speakers or headphones is retuned. It works inside the system’s own audio, so there’s nothing to set up in each app.

Compare

Before and after, one click

The big button switches between your new pitch and the original while the music keeps playing — no click, no gap. Left-click the gold ring by the clock to do the same from the tray.

Any pitch

Any pitch you want

Set A4 anywhere from 1 Hz to 20,000 Hz: step with − and + (hold to repeat), or double-click the number and type it. Prefer to think in C? Tune by C4 instead.

Spectrum

See where your tuning sits

Open the spectrum and every A (or C) of your tuning is marked, from the lowest octave to the highest. Maximise the window and it fills the screen.

In real time

Right on pitch

Every speaker’s clock runs a little fast or slow. 432hz measures yours and corrects for it, so the pitch comes out exactly where you set it — a 440 Hz tone really becomes 432 Hz at A4 = 432.

Out of the way

Out of sight

Close the window and 432hz keeps going from the tray (the menu bar on a Mac). Turn on Start with Windows (Open at login on a Mac) and every session starts at your pitch. Uninstall it and your sound is back exactly as it was.

MultiConsole

Studio effects on everything your PC plays, in real time.

Fig. 1 — Eleven live modules on one capsule Click to enlarge

MultiConsole is a small capsule that floats above every other window and runs your computer’s entire audio output through a chain of studio effects — YouTube, Spotify, games, the browser, anything. Autotune, saturation, compression, a band isolator, chorus, delay, reverb, a limiter, Mid/Side, stereo width and gain, all live, before the sound reaches your speakers or headphones.

Use it to study a reference: listen to only the mid, only the sides, or one narrow frequency band of any song you can stream. Or use it for fun: hard autotune a video, drown a stream in reverb, then hit Record and keep the result.

No DAW, no plugin host, no parameter pages. Almost every module is one knob — or one dial with two rings — and the knob’s centre shows you what it is doing while you turn it.

Whole system

Every app, one place

Whatever is playing on your PC passes through the same chain, so one setting works on a song, a video and a game alike.

One-knob modules

The details move with the knob

Turn up a compressor or a reverb and the ratio, make-up gain, room size and mix follow together. Every module starts neutral.

See it working

A live display in every knob

A spectrum, a stereo image, an echo timeline, a piano roll of corrected pitch — each knob’s dark centre shows its own effect as it happens.

Record

Keep what you hear

One button records the final output to a 24-bit stereo WAV file on your computer, without dropouts.

Always on top

Out of the way, never hidden

A pill-shaped window with adjustable background transparency, horizontal or vertical, that hides to the system tray.

Arrange on the capsule

Drag, remove, add back

Reorder the effect chain by dragging knobs on the capsule itself. A switched-off knob stays visible in grey, so its setting is never lost.

No clicks

Smooth bypass and changes

Bypass the whole chain with a 20 ms crossfade and come straight back — every change ramps instead of popping.

Honest latency

The real delay, shown live

Driver input and output, buffer and module delay are added up and displayed on the capsule. Settings save automatically.

What you’ll need

A way to route system audio into the app: the free VB-Audio Virtual Cable, or an audio interface with a loop-back feature. Set Windows to play into the cable, then pick the cable as MultiConsole’s input and your real speakers as its output — the settings panel walks you through it.

Visualize is a giant full-screen audio analyser for everything your PC plays. It listens to whatever your computer is playing — your browser, music player, games, your DAW — with no plugin and nothing to set up in each app.

Put it on a second screen while you mix, or full screen while you listen: the spectrum, loudness, stereo width, notes, key and chords are all there at a glance, and the buttons fade away when you stop moving the mouse.

Optional listen tools let you hear the mix in mono, only the parts that cancel in mono, or everything except them. They need VB-CABLE, a free virtual audio driver from another company; the analyser itself works without it.

Spectrum

Big, sharp spectrum

A full-screen spectrum with a waterfall of the last seconds scrolling underneath. Peak hold, a whole-song average, and a snapshot curve to compare against.

Notes, key, chords

Know what’s playing

The loudest peaks are labelled with their note, like “F1 · 44 Hz”. One click listens for about 8 seconds and estimates the song’s key; a chord box then shows the chord playing now and its place in the key.

Width // Freq

Stereo width per frequency

See how wide every frequency is — as a line, as dots (bigger dot, louder sound) or as a 3D cylinder you turn with the mouse. Sounds that cancel in mono show in red.

Colour

Colour by band or by sound

BANDS colours every graph by range, from orange bass to blue highs. BY START gives each sound that began at the same moment its own colour, across every graph.

Loudness

Loudness and true peak

Momentary, short-term and integrated loudness in LUFS, loudness range, peak-to-loudness ratio and true peak, measured the EBU R128 / ITU-R BS.1770 way. It starts over on its own when a new song begins.

Your way

Yours To Customize

Spectrum, waterfall or both; L+R, L / R or Mid / Side; smoothing, tilt and speed; scope, polar, band or history stereo pictures. F11 shows the picture alone over the whole screen.

Installing, your license code, and how to reach us.

Seeing “Windows protected your PC”?

When you open the Instrument Player installer, Windows may show a blue box that says “Windows protected your PC” and “Unknown publisher”. That is Windows SmartScreen. It appears for new apps that aren’t signed with a code-signing certificate yet and haven’t built up a history with Microsoft. Instrument Player is new, and our certificate is still being issued, so for now Windows can’t tell who made it.

To install it anyway, click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Only do this with the installer you downloaded from ybkaudio.com or from the link in our email.
